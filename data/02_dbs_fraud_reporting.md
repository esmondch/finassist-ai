## 1. Urgent contact information

DBS provides a 24-hour Fraud, Scam and Lost Cards Reporting hotline:

- Calling from Singapore: **1800 339 6963**
- Calling from overseas: **+65 6339 6963**

Customers who suspect fraud, scams, unauthorised transactions or compromised banking details should contact DBS promptly through this dedicated reporting channel.

Do not delay contacting DBS while trying to resolve an active incident through this academic chatbot.

## 2. General personal banking enquiries

The DBS contact page lists separate general enquiry numbers for DBS and POSB customers.

**DBS Personal Banking:**
- Singapore: **1800 111 1111**
- Overseas: **+65 6327 2265**

**POSB Personal Banking:**
- Singapore: **1800 339 6666**
- Overseas: **+65 6339 6666**

These are general personal banking enquiry numbers. They are different from the dedicated 24-hour Fraud, Scam and Lost Cards Reporting hotline.

The DBS contact page also provides digital support options, including digibot and its Message Us service.

**Contact channel clarification:**
- For general banking enquiries, identify whether the customer is asking for DBS or POSB contact details.
- For suspected scams, fraud, unauthorised transactions or compromised banking details, direct the customer to the dedicated fraud-reporting hotline.
- Do not substitute the general banking enquiry numbers for the dedicated fraud-reporting hotline, or vice versa.
- If the appropriate channel is unclear, direct the customer to the official DBS contact page.

## 3. If banking credentials or details may have been compromised

DBS warns customers not to disclose sensitive banking information to unknown individuals, websites or applications, including:

- Internet Banking credentials.
- One-Time Passwords (OTPs).
- Digital Token approvals.
- ATM, debit-card or credit-card details.

DBS also advises customers:

- Do not click links, call phone numbers or scan QR codes contained in unsolicited messages.
- Do not download applications from unknown or unofficial sources.
- Use official DBS websites and applications rather than links supplied by unsolicited callers or messages.

If a customer believes their banking details have been compromised, DBS advises contacting it immediately through its official fraud-reporting hotline.

## 4. Understanding the DBS Safety Switch

DBS describes Safety Switch as a security feature that allows customers to suspend access to certain banking services when they suspect they have become victims of a scam.

According to DBS, activating Safety Switch suspends:

- Access to digibank Online and digibank Mobile.
- Access to and use of PayLah! services.
- Physical and digital transactions through ATM, debit and credit cards.

DBS states that deposits into current and savings accounts are not affected.

**Important limitations:**

- Safety Switch does not automatically block incoming funds transfers.
- Existing pre-authorised GIRO arrangements are not automatically blocked.
- Customers can ask a DBS Customer Service Officer about extending the suspension to these functions.
- Customers should remain on the hotline after making a Safety Switch request so that a Customer Service Officer can follow up and assist.

If a customer reports an unauthorised debit- or credit-card transaction, DBS advises them to stay on the line for assistance from a Customer Service Officer.

FinAssist AI cannot activate Safety Switch, suspend banking services or verify whether a customer's request has been completed.

## 5. If the customer transferred money or made a transaction to a scammer

DBS's scam guidance directs customers to contact its 24-hour fraud/scam hotline:

- Singapore: **1800 339 6963**
- Overseas: **+65 6339 6963**

Customers may also visit a DBS/POSB branch for assistance.

The chatbot must not imply that a transfer can definitely be reversed, recovered or stopped. The outcome depends on the circumstances and DBS's handling of the case.

## 6. If the customer did not share banking details and did not make a payment

DBS's scam guidance describes ScamShield as a channel for reporting potential scam attempts where banking details have not been compromised and no transaction has been made to the scammer.

The guidance refers customers to:

- The ScamShield app.
- The ScamShield helpline: **1799**.

This is distinct from reporting a compromised DBS account or a payment already made to a scammer. For suspected account compromise, fraud or unauthorised transactions, direct customers to DBS's official fraud-reporting hotline.

## 7. Safe response principles

When handling a suspected scam or fraud question, FinAssist AI should:

1. Prioritise immediate contact with DBS through the official fraud-reporting hotline.
2. Remind customers not to share passwords, PINs, OTPs, Digital Token approvals or full card details in the chat.
3. Avoid asking customers to click links or call numbers from suspicious messages.
4. Never claim that the chatbot has blocked an account, activated Safety Switch, reported a scam or recovered funds.
5. Never promise reimbursement, recovery or a particular investigation outcome.
6. Distinguish general banking enquiries from urgent fraud and lost-card reporting.
7. Explain the limitations of Safety Switch when describing what it suspends.
