## 1. First check whether the transaction is genuinely unfamiliar

Before reporting a transaction as fraud, DBS asks customers to verify several details.

### Merchant name

A merchant may use a different name for payment processing. The name on a card statement may therefore differ from the shop, service or brand the customer recognises.

### Transaction date

The date displayed on a card statement may differ from the date the purchase was made. DBS notes that overseas merchants may record a transaction on a different date because of time-zone differences.

### Supplementary cardholders and family members

Check whether a supplementary cardholder or a family member who was previously given the card details made the transaction.

These checks do not mean that an unfamiliar transaction is safe. If the customer establishes that the transaction was not theirs, DBS says to report it promptly.

## 2. Fraud dispute for an unauthorised credit-card transaction

The DBS guide describes using the digibank mobile app to report a credit-card fraud dispute.

The documented mobile-app route begins with:

**More > Manage Cards & Loans > Report Fraud**

The published flow includes:

1. Log in to the official digibank mobile app using the bank's authentication process.
2. Tap **More** and, under **Manage Cards & Loans**, select **Report Fraud**.
3. Select the option to report fraud or submit a transaction dispute.
4. Select the transaction or transactions being reported.
5. Review and confirm the submission.
6. Follow the app's instructions for blocking and replacing the card, where applicable.

The guide states that a card blocked through the described fraud-reporting flow cannot be unblocked through that process. Customers should read the confirmation screen carefully before proceeding.

The guide states that up to **15 transactions** may be selected in one submission. If there are more than 15, a second submission may be required for the same card.

**Important scope and navigation notes:**

- This documented process applies to the credit-card fraud-reporting flow described in the DBS guide.
- Do not assume that the same in-app process applies to every debit-card dispute or merchant dispute.
- Do not invent alternative menu paths or app options that are not supported by the retrieved DBS information.
- App navigation may change. Treat the current instructions shown in the official app as authoritative.
- FinAssist AI cannot submit the dispute or block the card.

## 3. Fraud reporting for debit-card transactions

For an unauthorised debit-card transaction, the DBS guide directs customers to contact DBS immediately through its fraud-reporting hotline:

- Singapore: **1800 339 6963**
- Overseas: **+65 6339 6963**

Do not tell a customer that the credit-card in-app workflow necessarily applies to debit-card disputes.

## 4. Transactions that may not qualify for a fraud dispute

The DBS guide explains that some transaction types may be ineligible for a fraud dispute or chargeback. Its examples include:

- Authenticated online transactions using 3D Secure (3DS).
- EMV chip transactions.
- Contactless transactions, including transactions through mobile wallets.
- Annual or monthly membership and subscription transactions.
- Transactions involving goods or services that the customer authorised or participated in, where the issue is with the merchant.

For a merchant dispute, such as goods not received or a product or service problem, DBS advises customers to communicate with the merchant directly before contacting the bank.

**Important:** Do not turn these examples into an absolute eligibility decision. The full DBS guide and applicable card-scheme rules govern the case. Direct customers to DBS for an assessment.

## 5. Understanding a pending card transaction

DBS explains that a pending transaction means the card payment has been approved and a temporary hold has been placed on the customer's funds or available credit. It may become a final charge after the merchant completes the sale.

The DBS guide states:

- A pending transaction cannot be stopped or cancelled by the bank once it has been approved.
- A customer seeking to cancel a purchase should contact the merchant.
- If the pending transaction does not get posted, the customer will not be billed for it.
- If it is posted, the relevant dispute process can then apply.
- DBS says it will monitor reported transactions and act once they are posted to the account.

**Important distinctions:**

- A pending transaction is not the same as a posted transaction.
- Do not describe a pending transaction as a completed charge.
- Do not promise that a pending transaction will disappear by a particular date.
- Do not invent a settlement date or a specific time for the release of a temporary hold.
- The available information does not establish that the bank can cancel an approved pending transaction.
- If the retrieved passages do not explain what happens in a particular case, state that the available information does not specify it.

## 6. Dispute status, temporary credits and investigation

The DBS guide describes several possible dispute statuses and outcomes. It states that an investigation process may take **up to 60 days**.

The guide also describes scenarios where a temporary credit may be applied while an investigation is ongoing. It explains that the temporary credit may be reversed if, for example, the merchant refunds the amount or the transaction is found to be genuine and made by the cardholder.

The guide describes different possible outcomes for authenticated transactions and other transaction types.

**Important distinctions:**

- An investigation taking up to 60 days does not mean every dispute will be resolved within exactly 60 days.
- A temporary credit is not guaranteed.
- A successful dispute or reimbursement is not guaranteed.
- Do not claim that DBS will approve, reject, reverse or refund a particular transaction.
- Do not invent internal investigation steps or claim that every reported transaction follows the same sequence.
- Explain that the outcome depends on the transaction type and DBS's investigation.

The available information does not establish a guaranteed refund, reversal or temporary-credit outcome for every dispute.

## 7. Information customers may find useful when speaking to DBS

The DBS guide advises customers to verify transaction details before reporting an unfamiliar transaction.

For a conversation with DBS, a customer may find it useful to identify:

- The transaction date shown in the app or statement.
- The transaction amount and currency.
- The merchant description shown by DBS.
- Whether the transaction is pending or posted.
- Whether a supplementary cardholder or family member may have used the card.
- Whether the customer recognises the merchant or authorised the purchase.

**Important distinction:** This is a list of potentially useful transaction details, not a DBS-mandated document checklist. The available summary does not establish that customers must provide a particular reference-number format, receipt, screenshot or other document in every dispute.

If DBS requires additional information, customers should follow the instructions given through its official channels.
